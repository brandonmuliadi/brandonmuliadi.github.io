Inspired by the peerless Dynomight's [Underrated reasons to be thankful](https://dynomight.net/thanks/) series.

Not included, despite being awesome, due to already being generally appropriately rated (at least around me): liberal democracy, economic growth, increasing acceptance of LGBTQ+ folks, and much more.

1. That "overmorrow" and "ereyesterday" (meaning "the day after tomorrow" and "the day before yesterday", respectively).
1. That "crine" is a real word, meaning to shrink or shrivel.
1. That the plural of "moose" isn't "meese".[^1]
1. That protein powder was a niche and rather unpleasant product 50 years ago, and now it's literally everywhere[^2] and has a nice neutral taste.
1. That farmers' markets.
1. That iodized salt, preventing iodine deficiency and impaired childhood brain development for pennies per person per year.
1. That people's sensitivity to bitterness usually decreases after adolescence, which is great since vegetables make food so much better (or at least that's what I noticed once I could tolerate the bitterness).[^3]
1. That flaxseeds have a crap ton of ALA and you can put them in bread and oatmeal and everything.
1. That parents are no longer regularly advised to avoid hugging, kissing, or otherwise coddling their babies, which was the dominant position through the 1930s in the US and much of the West.[^4]
1. That beauty standards vary widely across time and place, and a lot of [past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_binding) [beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohaguro) [standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_cranial_deformation) are strange by dominant Western beauty standards[^5], so beauty standards are kinda arbitrary and beauty is in the eye of the beholder and so on, how about I say that if you're reading this you're beautiful?
1. That a lot of fruits and vegetables we eat, like almonds, avocados, bananas, beets, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, carrots, cauliflower, corn, cucumbers, eggplants, kale, kohlrabi, lettuce, melons, peaches, and potatoes are really not very tasty (or like 99% inedible seed, or toxic) in their wild forms, and we successfully bred them to be tasty (and less than 99% inedible seed, and nontoxic).
1. That fleshy fruits were optimized by natural selection to be eaten, and as a result some are actually quite tasty in their wild forms, who needs selective breeding?[^6]
1. That humans have extremely strong stomach acid, approaching that of scavengers like vultures, which has probably saved a lot of humans from food poisoning in the course of history.
1. That humans aren't obligate carnivores.
1. That humans can and do eat a huge variety of foods, thanks to being omnivores, our ways of food processing, and modern supply chains.
1. That close to sunrise and sunset (depending on season and latitude), the sun hits us at an angle such that most of its UVB radiation is absorbed by the ozone layer, significantly reducing the risk of skin damage.
1. That in 2018, Coefficient Giving (then Open Philanthropy) gave an $11,367,500 grant to David Baker and Neil King's research on computational protein design and a universal flu vaccine (the latter of which the NIH had refused to fund), and in 2024 Neil King's lab got $70 million in research funding and David Baker won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and King said that getting the grant was "[literally life or death for our flu program](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/how-neil-king-and-david-baker-are-using-ai-to-create-more-effective-vaccines/)".
1. That people generally [completely bounce back from divorce and (if they find a new job) layoffs](https://academic.oup.com/ej/article-abstract/118/529/F222/5088790).
1. [That light-touch interventions don't typically change the world](https://regressiontothemeat.substack.com/p/i-am-grateful-that-light-touch-interventions).
1. That legume pastas are becoming mainstream.
1. That in one [study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2023.112813) (n=175), participants ranked the Impossible burger at least as highly as a 100% beef burger, regardless of whether they were informed which burger was which.[^7]
1. That cage-free eggs cost only a couple of cents more per egg to produce than caged eggs ([this study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4990890/) from 2015 estimates $0.02), and although at the grocery store the difference in cost between cage-free and caged eggs is often a lot more (willingness to pay and all that), we can expect the markup to decrease as cage-free becomes the baseline and gets commoditized, and [we've seen this happen](https://faunalytics.org/specialty-egg-premiums-affected-by-market-shocks/) in states with cage-free laws.[^8]
1. That the normal distribution and central limit theorem.
1. That the Poisson distribution and Poisson processes.
1. That holomorphic functions.
1. That the fundamental theorem of algebra.
1. That arithmetic modulo a prime.
1. That the triangle, the strongest shape, and the endless patterns in triangle geometry.
    - That the three medians of a triangle meet at a point, the three internal angle bisectors meet at a point, the three perpendicular bisectors meet at a point, and the three lines containing the altitudes meet at a point.
1. That proofs without words.
1. That [The Asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Asylum), because what is society without mockbusters?
    - Just look at this. ![Absolute cinema](/assets/images/sharknado.gif)

[^1]: Uncertain about this one; maybe it would be better. Also, TIL that "mongoose" comes from Marathi (hence "mongeese" is incorrect).
[^2]: Maybe it's going too far. I mean, do we really need protein fruit punch, protein Pop-Tarts, and protein popcorn? On the other hand, I think protein Cheerios would be a great idea if they would just make a plain flavor. Please General Mills, I need this.
[^3]: Still can't eat bitter melon, though.
[^4]: The reasoning being that it could spoil them, and make them dependent and emotionally weak as adults.
[^5]: To be clear, I don't think these practices are *objectively* strange, although footbinding seems objectively painful and debilitating. Plenty of Western beauty standards or cultural practices are or would be weird to people from other cultures and times. My point is that it's all subjective!
[^6]: Shoutout to wild blueberries and wild strawberries.
[^7]: Interestingly, in the blind condition, participants were generally able to identify which burger was the beef burger, but still ranked the Impossible burger at least as highly. To be fair, the beef burger came from a Cornell dining hall, and was more likely to be described as "dry" (or, in the blind tasting, "bland").
[^8]: I acknowledge that many cage-free hens still suffer horribly. My best guess is that they live better lives on net? At the very least, cage-free laws and commitments are a first step towards more-humane systems?
