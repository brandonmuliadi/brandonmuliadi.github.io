Idea (and writing style) lifted wholesale from the peerless Dynomight's "Underrated reasons to be thankful" series.

1. That in 2018, Coefficient Giving (then Open Philanthropy) gave a $11,367,500 grant to David Baker and Neil King's research on computational protein design and a universal flu vaccine, which the NIH had refused to fund, and in 2024 Neil King's lab got $70 million in research funding and David Baker won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and King said that getting the grant was "literally life and death for our flu program".
2. That in at least one [study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37254388/) (n=175), "in the blind condition, there was no significant difference in preferences between the beef burger and the hybrid and pea protein burgers", and in the informed condition the Impossible burger actually beat the beef burger.
3. That chickpea pasta is becoming mainstream, which is great because legumes are nutritious and a great source of plant-based protein and in 2018 less than one in five Americans consumed 1.5 cups a week, the amount recommended by the 2020-2025 USDA Dietary Guidelines.
4. That Sprouts sometimes has [pea protein powder](https://shop.sprouts.com/store/sprouts/products/20347127-80-pea-isolate-protein-powder-per-lb) on sale for $14/lb, and I know it used to be $5/lb in 2022 and $10/lb in early 2025 and I'm mad about this too, and $14 for enough protein powder for 20 bowls of protein oatmeal is not that bad. 
6. That protein powder used to be a niche and rather unpleasant product 50 years ago, and now it's available everywhere and has a much more neutral flavor.
5. That farmers' markets (no, Sprouts doesn't count).
7. That your sensitivity to bitterness goes down over time after adolescence, which is great since vegetables make a lot of dishes so much better (or at least I noticed this once I started being able to tolerate the bitterness).
8. That gratitude journalling works.
9. That cage-free eggs cost like $0.03 more to produce than conventional eggs, and 
6. That flaxseeds have a crap ton of ALAs and you can put them in bread and oatmeal and everything.

5. That libraries.
