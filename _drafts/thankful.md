Idea (and writing style) lifted wholesale from the peerless Dynomight's "Underrated reasons to be thankful" series.

1. That in 2018, Coefficient Giving (then Open Philanthropy) gave a $11,367,500 grant to David Baker and Neil King's research on computational protein design and a universal flu vaccine, which the NIH had refused to fund, and in 2024 Neil King's lab got $70 million in research funding and David Baker won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and King said that getting the grant was "literally life and death for our flu program".
2. That in at least one [study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37254388/) (n=175), "in the blind condition, there was no significant difference in preferences between the beef burger and the hybrid and pea protein burgers", and in the informed condition the Impossible burger actually beat the beef burger.
3. That chickpea pasta is becoming mainstream, which is great because legumes are nutritious and a great source of plant-based protein and in 2018 less than one in five Americans consumed 1.5 cups a week, the amount recommended by the 2020-2025 USDA Dietary Guidelines.
4. That protein powder used to be a niche and rather unpleasant product 50 years ago, and now it's available everywhere and has a much more neutral flavor.
5. That farmers' markets (no, Sprouts doesn't count).
7. That your sensitivity to bitterness goes down over time after adolescence, which is great since vegetables make a lot of dishes so much better (or at least that's what I noticed once I could tolerate the bitterness).
8. That cage-free eggs cost like $0.03 more to produce than conventional eggs, and 
9. That flaxseeds have a crap ton of ALAs and you can put them in bread and oatmeal and everything.
10. That the plural of "moose" isn't "meese". (Uncertain)
11. That beauty standards are pretty arbitrary, it would be pretty bad if attractiveness could be reduced to a single number, and also have you seen beauty standards from thousands of years ago?
12. That fleshy fruits were optimized by natural selection to be eaten by us, and are really tasty.
13. That a lot of plants we eat, like cabbage, kale, broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, kohlrabi, bananas, almonds, watermelons, and carrots, are really not very tasty in their wild forms, and we've successfully bred them to be tasty.
13. That within a few hours of sunrise and sunset, the sun hits us at an angle such that most of its UV radiation is absorbed by the ozone layer, so you can enjoy the sun without risk of skin damage.
14. That humans have extremely strong stomach acid, approaching that of scavengers like vultures, which has probably saved a lot of humans from food poisoning in the course of history.
14. That humans aren't obligate carnivores.
15. That humans can (and do) eat a huge variety of foods, thanks to being omnivores, our ways of food processing, and modern supply chains.
16. That supply chains.
17. That the normal distribution.
18. That time; I'm glad the world isn't static and unchanging.
19. [That light touch interventions don't change the world](regressiontothemeat.substack.com/p/i-am-grateful-that-light-touch-interventions).
